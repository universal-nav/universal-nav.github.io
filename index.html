UNI: Collecting Sidewalk Navigation Demonstrations Without a Robot

Anonymous Authors
Under review at ICRA 2027

Code, dataset, and hardware specification will be released upon acceptance.

UNI is an instrumented rollator walker that collects sidewalk navigation demonstrations with a smartphone, no robot required.

Abstract

Learning sidewalk navigation from demonstration is bottlenecked by the cost of collecting demonstrations, which conventionally requires teleoperating a robot through public pedestrian space. We remove the robot from the data collection loop.

UNI is a rollator walker instrumented with a consumer smartphone, which an operator pushes along ordinary sidewalks. Each session records RGB video, LiDAR depth, and device pose, from which we recover metric waypoints in the robot's frame of reference. We use it to collect UNI-Sidewalk, a corpus of sidewalk navigation demonstrations, and we deploy a policy trained on that corpus on a physical robot.

The feasibility filter

A pedestrian and a wheeled base do not see the same sidewalk. At the intersection below, the pedestrian route crosses a curb that the walker cannot; the walker route diverges to the curb ramp. Demonstrations collected on wheels inherit this constraint for free, which is what makes a walker a better data collection platform than a person with a head-mounted camera.

On-robot deployment
